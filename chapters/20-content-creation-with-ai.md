# 20 · Create Content with AI: Earn Attention by Being Useful

**A thousand posts can disappear into the feed. One clear answer to a real problem can make someone remember your work.**

AI can speed up research organization, drafts, editing and repurposing. The creator still chooses what is worth saying and verifies the claims. Start with an audience and a useful outcome, not a demand for “viral content.” A hook earns the next sentence; the rest of the piece must earn the reader's trust.

## Setup

Create a content brief with audience, question, evidence, format, tone and desired action. Keep a source ledger and an asset-rights ledger. For a developer audience, a real bug fix, before/after screenshot and reproducible repository can be stronger than broad claims about changing the world.

Use a text model for structure and editing, a vision model to critique images you are allowed to share, and suitable media tools for production. Choose models through the [setup guide](31-models-and-workspace.md). Avoid uploading confidential client material or claiming that generated scenes depict actual events.

## Build one strong piece

Choose a narrow question: “Why did my Three.js demo require a local server, and how did I package it to work offline?” Gather the relevant code, build output and limitations. Write the answer yourself in rough form. Ask AI to remove repetition, identify unexplained terms and propose three truthful opening lines.

A useful hook might be: “The game worked on my laptop, but the downloaded HTML was blank. The missing piece was the build, not another game server.” The body should then demonstrate the distinction between source modules, a static bundle and an application backend. End with a clear way to try the build.

| Stage | Deliverable | Quality question |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Source and example notes | Can I verify the central claim? |
| Draft | One complete explanation | Does each paragraph advance the answer? |
| Edit | Clear, concise version | Are claims stronger than the evidence? |
| Package | Title, image and alt text | Does the preview match the content? |
| Publish | Accessible final piece | Can the reader use the result? |
| Review | Reader feedback and corrections | What confused people? |

## Repurpose without becoming repetitive

Turn the same evidence into a short post, a practical tutorial and a demonstration video, but adapt each format to its audience. A short post should not pretend to contain every detail. A tutorial needs prerequisites and error recovery. A video needs visible actions rather than a voice reading the article.

Prompt: “Edit this draft for developers who know basic JavaScript. Preserve my claims and examples. Mark unsupported statements instead of inventing evidence. Suggest a stronger opening that the body actually proves. Keep the voice natural and avoid exaggerated marketing language.”

## Challenges and practical project

Generic output usually begins with a generic brief. Add a real example, audience constraint and point of view. Factual drift appears when every revision invents new supporting details; maintain a locked fact sheet. Engagement metrics can reward outrage or ambiguity; also measure useful replies, tutorial completions and corrections. Never manufacture testimonials, credentials, screenshots or customer results.

Create a three-piece series from one completed project. Include a demo, a lesson learned and a reproducible guide. Track what people ask after reading, then improve the original. This workflow supports education, product communication and community building. Your content becomes a trail of useful work rather than a collection of claims about your potential.

Before publishing, check names, numbers, links, permissions, accessibility and whether an ordinary reader could mistake a synthetic example for a real outcome. Give credit for collaborators' work. If something is wrong, correct it visibly. A reliable creator can build trust even with a small audience.
