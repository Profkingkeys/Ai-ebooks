# 19 · Study with AI: Turn “I Understand” into “I Can Recall and Apply”

**The explanation felt clear. Then the exam question arrived, and the idea disappeared. Recognition was never the same as mastery.**

AI can provide practice, feedback and alternative explanations, but it cannot guarantee A grades. The useful goal is independent performance on the learning objectives. Start with your lecturer's material, course scope and assessment rules. A model's wider knowledge can be interesting while still being outside the syllabus or incorrect.

## Setup and model choice

Create a folder for one course, with authorized lecture notes, learning objectives, a question log and a revision calendar. Use a text model that can work from the supplied material and quote short supporting passages accurately. If the notes contain diagrams, check that a vision-capable model can read labels correctly. Do not upload classmates' personal information or restricted assessment material.

Use a source-bounded instruction: “Teach only from the notes I provide unless I explicitly ask for external context. Mark anything absent from the notes. Ask one question, wait for my answer, then explain the error with a reference to the relevant section.” This is especially useful when a course uses definitions or conventions that differ from other textbooks.

## Build a learning cycle

Read a small section, close it, and explain the idea from memory. Compare your explanation with the source. Turn gaps into short questions. Revisit them on later days and vary the examples. The [Learning Scientists' study materials](https://www.learningscientists.org/downloadable-materials) introduce approaches including retrieval practice and spaced practice; use them as strategies to test in your own study routine, not a promise of a particular grade.

For a physiology topic, do more than memorize a list of autonomic divisions. Draw the relationship from memory, explain what changes in a specific scenario, and identify where your explanation depends on a receptor or pathway. Ask AI to challenge a misconception rather than simply produce another long note.

| Error type | What it looks like | Next practice |
|---|---|---|
| Missing fact | Cannot recall a definition | Short retrieval question |
| Confused relationship | Reverses cause and effect | Explain a diagram from memory |
| Calculation error | Correct method, wrong arithmetic | Check units and redo a fresh example |
| Transfer failure | Knows the example but not a new case | Solve a different scenario |
| Misread question | Answers the wrong task | Underline the requested operation |

## A realistic weekly workflow

After a lecture, spend a short session writing questions while the material is fresh. Later, answer them without the notes. At the weekend, mix older and newer questions so the topic label does not give away the method. Reserve time for difficult areas and recovery; a revision schedule that assumes endless concentration will not survive a real week.

For calculations, ask the model to show units and assumptions, then verify the arithmetic independently. For essays, write your own outline before requesting feedback. For group study, rotate who explains and who checks the source. Respect your institution's AI policy and never submit generated work as your own when that is prohibited.

## Practical project and troubleshooting

Build a twenty-question bank tied to course objectives. Have a classmate or lecturer review a sample for correctness. Record first-attempt answers and revisit errors after a delay. Measure improvement on fresh questions, not on items whose wording you memorized. Keep a list of AI mistakes so you learn where the tool needs supervision.

If answers conflict with your notes, inspect the exact definition and context. If the model makes every question easy, specify the cognitive skill: explain, compare, calculate or apply. If studying becomes passive, close the chat and solve a problem on paper. Your final portfolio artifact can be a source-linked study template and an honest reflection, with no private grades or classmates' records exposed.
