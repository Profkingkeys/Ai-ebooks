# 24 · Cook with AI: Learn the Technique, Taste the Result

**A recipe can be beautifully written and still burn in your pan. Good cooking depends on observing what is actually happening.**

AI can help plan meals, scale quantities, explain techniques and adapt a recipe to available ingredients. It cannot smell spoilage, reliably identify allergens from a photo or verify that food reached a safe temperature. Use it as a planning assistant while you remain attentive in the kitchen.

Africa, the Americas and Asia contain many distinct food traditions. The three beginner dishes below are representative practice projects, not definitive recipes for entire continents. Ask cooks from the relevant community about regional variations and acknowledge adaptations honestly.

## Setup and safety

Have a clean work area, measuring tools, a suitable pan, a timer and a food thermometer when cooking foods that need one. List dietary restrictions and known allergens before requesting substitutions. Check ingredient packaging; AI guesses are not an allergen control. Separate raw meat from ready-to-eat food and follow official storage guidance.

[FoodSafety.gov's temperature chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures) specifies 74°C/165°F for poultry and reheated leftovers. Measure at an appropriate thick part rather than judging by color. The [CDC handwashing guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/about/) supports washing before food preparation and after handling contamination risks. These checks matter more than a model's description of how the finished dish should look.

## Practice 1: a simple West African-inspired tomato rice

For approximately four modest servings, prepare two cups of long-grain rice, three medium tomatoes, one red pepper, one onion, two tablespoons of tomato paste, three tablespoons of oil, stock or water, and seasoning to taste. Optional heat should suit the people eating. This is a simplified jollof-inspired learning recipe; regional cooks use different ingredients and methods.

Blend the tomatoes, pepper and half the onion. Slice the remaining onion. Warm the oil, soften the sliced onion, stir in the paste and cook briefly without scorching. Add the blended mixture and cook, stirring, until it thickens and loses its raw aroma. Add the rinsed rice and the amount of liquid appropriate to that rice's package directions, accounting for liquid already in the sauce. Cover and cook gently. Check near the expected cooking time; add a little hot water if the rice is still firm and the pan is dry. Rest covered off the heat, then fluff and taste.

Ask AI why the rice became mushy or scorched, providing the pan, rice type, heat, liquid and timing. Do not merely ask for a “better recipe.” Your variables make diagnosis useful.

## Practice 2: American-style sheet-pan chicken and vegetables

Use about 600 g of chicken pieces of similar size, potatoes cut into small even pieces, carrots, a little oil and seasonings you enjoy. Heat the oven to around 200°C. Toss vegetables with oil and seasoning, arrange with space around the chicken, and roast. Timing varies substantially with cut, size and oven; use the thermometer to confirm poultry reaches 74°C/165°F. If vegetables need longer, continue cooking them separately after safely cooked chicken is removed.

Keep raw-chicken utensils and juices away from cooked food. Ask AI to organize the preparation sequence so clean tasks happen before raw-meat handling, but inspect the plan yourself. The lesson is coordination and temperature verification, not memorizing one universal roasting time.

## Practice 3: an East Asian-inspired ginger tofu stir-fry

For two servings, prepare about 250 g firm tofu, two cups of sliced vegetables, a small piece of grated ginger, a garlic clove, one tablespoon of soy sauce, a splash of water and a little cooking oil. Soy is an allergen and many soy sauces contain wheat; use a suitable verified alternative if needed.

Drain and pat the tofu dry, then cut it into even pieces. Prepare all vegetables and sauce before heating the pan. Brown tofu in a lightly oiled pan, remove it, then cook the firmer vegetables before softer ones. Add ginger and garlic briefly, return the tofu, and toss with the sauce until heated through. Serve with freshly prepared rice or another suitable accompaniment. Avoid overcrowding, which can turn a quick stir-fry into steaming.

## Use AI to improve technique

| Problem | Information to provide | Useful experiment |
|---|---|---|
| Rice too wet | Rice type, liquid, lid and heat | Adjust one variable next time |
| Uneven roasting | Piece sizes and tray layout | Cut evenly and avoid crowding |
| Watery stir-fry | Pan size and vegetable moisture | Dry ingredients and cook in batches |
| Unbalanced flavor | Measured seasoning and serving size | Make small, tasted adjustments |

A text model can scale a shopping list and explain sequence. A vision model can comment on presentation but cannot certify food safety. Never rely on AI to decide whether an unknown wild plant, mushroom or spoiled ingredient is safe to eat.

## Your cooking portfolio

Cook each dish, record substitutions and photograph your own result. Write what changed between attempt one and two. Build a weekly plan that fits your budget, equipment and household preferences. Verify storage and reheating instructions against official guidance rather than improvising them from a generated answer. The outcome is practical judgment: knowing what to observe, when to adjust and when a safety check takes priority over appearance.
