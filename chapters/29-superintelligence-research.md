# 29 · Superintelligence: Study the Possibility Without Pretending It Is Solved

**The phrase “smarter than every human” is a claim about extraordinary capability. It is not an installation command.**

Superintelligence is commonly discussed as intelligence exceeding human performance across a broad range of important domains, but definitions and measurement remain unsettled. This guide does not offer a proven construction recipe, a guaranteed timeline or a method for uncontrolled self-improvement. It offers a disciplined way to investigate capabilities and the conditions under which more capable systems could be evaluated responsibly.

## Begin with questions that can be tested

What tasks are being compared? Which humans or teams form the baseline? How much time, data, compute and tool access does each receive? Does the system generalize beyond familiar tests? Is its performance reliable enough to matter in the intended setting? Without these details, “superhuman” can be an impressive word attached to an unfair comparison.

A system may exceed human performance on one narrow task while remaining unreliable elsewhere. Faster code generation does not establish scientific judgment, trustworthy long-term planning or safe real-world agency. Separate these properties instead of treating one benchmark as a universal intelligence score.

## Setup for responsible research

Use the [AGI research roadmap](28-agi-research-roadmap.md) first. Build reproducible evaluations and understand existing models before considering speculative architectures. Use isolated environments, limited tools, explicit budgets and external controls. Do not give an experimental system unrestricted access to money, infrastructure, sensitive data or its own safeguards.

Keep capability experiments separate from governance decisions. An evaluation result may justify further study, not deployment. Independent reviewers should be able to inspect methods and failures. The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) is a governance reference, not a certificate that an advanced system is safe.

## A useful research agenda

| Area | Concrete starting project | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluation | New tasks resistant to simple memorization | Baselines, provenance and repeatability |
| Reliability | Bounded planning with checked constraints | Failure rate and recovery behavior |
| Interpretability | Study a small model or known circuit | Reproducible observations and limits |
| Oversight | Compare human review procedures | Errors caught, missed and introduced |
| Control | Test external stop and permission mechanisms | Whether limits remain effective |
| Social impact | Analyze a defined deployment scenario | Affected groups and uncertainty |

Choose one area. A small rigorous project beats a diagram connecting every fashionable component. For example, compare two review interfaces for detecting errors in AI-generated analyses. Use synthetic data, define the task, obtain appropriate consent for any human study and report uncertainty. That can improve oversight without claiming to create superintelligence.

## Understand the hard problems

A model can optimize the wrong objective, exploit a flawed reward, appear successful under weak evaluation or behave differently when conditions change. More model-generated explanations do not automatically solve these problems. An explanation can itself be incomplete or misleading. Use independent measurements, adversarial cases and controls that do not depend on the model choosing to obey.

If you study automated improvement, keep changes in a sandbox and require external review before promotion. Preserve the baseline and evaluate on unseen tasks. Do not allow an experiment to rewrite its own success criteria, remove logging or expand permissions merely because it reports that doing so would help.

## Practical outcome and future steps

Produce a research proposal with a narrow question, baseline, evaluation set, resource budget, risk analysis and stopping conditions. Run the bounded experiment and publish both positive and negative results. Clearly label speculation in the discussion section.

The motivation is serious: advanced AI might help with scientific discovery, resource planning and other difficult problems. That possibility does not make every proposed architecture effective or every deployment wise. The strongest contribution is evidence that helps the field distinguish real progress from persuasive stories. Ambition and intellectual honesty belong together.
