# 08 - AI and the Economy

Economics is where AI stops being a feature and becomes a coordination problem.

A technology can be powerful and still have a small economic effect if it is expensive, difficult to deploy or trapped in a narrow workflow.

Economic effect expands when capability, cost, usability and trust move together.

## Productivity is not the whole story

Suppose an engineer writes code twice as fast.

That does not guarantee twice as much value.

The bottleneck may move.

Code can become cheap while product decisions, testing, deployment, compliance or distribution remain slow.

AI often removes one bottleneck and exposes another.

## The cost structure changes

AI products add:

- inference
- retrieval
- evaluation
- provider switching
- latency
- human review

Measure:

**cost per successful task**

not only:

**cost per token**

A cheap model that creates expensive corrections may be the expensive model.

## Labor substitution versus amplification

AI tends to be strongest where work is:

- repetitive
- language-heavy
- pattern-rich
- digitally observable
- easy to evaluate

It is harder where work depends heavily on:

- physical presence
- trust
- complex social negotiation
- direct manipulation of objects

Tasks may change before jobs change.

Jobs are bundles of tasks.

## AI-native companies

A traditional workflow:

human -> software -> human

An AI-native workflow:

human -> agent -> tools -> agent -> human

This can increase leverage.

It can also increase operational responsibility.

A small team can run software whose automated actions affect thousands of customers.

## What becomes scarce

As generated information becomes abundant, other resources become more valuable:

- trusted identity
- verified provenance
- customer relationships
- distribution
- compute
- proprietary real-world data
- regulatory permission
- human judgment

That is one of the deepest economic questions raised by AI.
