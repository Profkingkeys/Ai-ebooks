# 17 · Bible Study and Prayer with AI: Let the Tool Point Back to Scripture

**The answer may sound spiritual and still quote a verse that does not exist. Open the Bible before accepting the paragraph.**

For a Christian, AI can help organize study, compare a supplied passage, create revision questions and draft language for reflection. It is a tool for reading and writing, not God, a spiritual authority or a replacement for prayer, church community and mature pastoral counsel. This guide keeps the biblical text visible throughout the process.

## Set up a source-led study

Choose a Bible translation you are permitted to use and identify it in your notes. Read the complete passage before asking for a summary. Record book, chapter, verse range, surrounding argument and the questions you genuinely have. Some translations restrict reproduction; link to authorized editions rather than copying large amounts. [Project Gutenberg's King James Bible edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10) provides a text source, with rights and jurisdiction notices readers should check.

A capable text model is enough for the exercises. Retrieval from the exact passage is more useful than an impressive model inventing context. Ask the assistant to label observations from the text, historical claims needing sources, interpretive possibilities and personal applications separately. Where Christian traditions differ, ask it to identify the difference without presenting one tradition's interpretation as universal consensus.

## Work through a passage

Take Luke 10:25–37. First describe what the narrative explicitly says: a question is asked, a conversation follows, and Jesus tells a story. Then examine the surrounding verses and the question answered by the story. Only after observation should you discuss interpretation and application. Check any claim about first-century customs against a reputable commentary or historical source.

A useful prompt is:

> Using only the passage I provide, list five observations with verse references. Then suggest three questions for deeper study. Separate direct textual statements from interpretation. Do not invent quotations, Greek meanings or messages from God. If an answer needs outside context, say what source should be consulted.

Now close the model response and write your own summary. Compare it with the passage. This protects you from merely recognizing someone else's explanation without understanding the text.

| Step | Your action | AI's limited role |
|---|---|---|
| Read | Attend to the complete passage | Organize questions |
| Observe | Identify repeated words and relationships | Point to supplied verses |
| Interpret | Study context and consult sound teaching | Compare explicitly labeled readings |
| Apply | Consider faithful action in your circumstances | Help phrase reflection questions |
| Pray | Speak personally to God | Draft optional wording for you to revise |

## Pray in your own voice

AI can help someone who struggles to find words, but prayer is not a prompt that guarantees a particular outcome. A simple original draft might be: “Lord, help me understand Your Word, recognize where I need to grow, and act with love toward the people I meet today. Give me wisdom to seek help and courage to do what is right. Amen.” Adapt or discard it; the tool does not pray on your behalf or know God's private intentions for you.

Do not ask a model to declare that illness reflects insufficient faith, identify a person as spiritually dangerous, or prescribe fasting practices that ignore health needs. Bring serious spiritual concerns to trusted pastoral care and health concerns to appropriate professionals.

## A seven-day project

Choose one short passage. On day one, read it aloud and record questions. On day two, observe its structure. On day three, consult a trusted study resource. On day four, compare your interpretation with the passage. On day five, discuss it with a mature Christian or study group. On day six, write a personal application. On day seven, review what you learned and pray in your own words.

The outcome is a study journal with verified references and honest questions. If AI supplies a nonexistent verse, remove it and note the error. If it flattens a difficult doctrine into a slogan, return to the broader scriptural context. The strongest use of the technology is to support attention, understanding and faithful practice—not to make the tool the center of devotion.
