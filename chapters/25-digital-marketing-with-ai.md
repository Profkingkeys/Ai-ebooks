# 25 · Digital Marketing with AI: Measure the Customer's Decision

**A campaign can get thousands of clicks and still fail to help the business. Start with the decision you need to improve.**

AI can support audience research, copy variations, content planning and analysis. It cannot guarantee sales or make an unwanted product valuable. Becoming excellent at marketing means understanding customers, making truthful offers, testing carefully and learning from results.

## Setup

Choose one product, audience and conversion event. The event might be a qualified inquiry, completed lesson or legitimate purchase—not merely an impression. Map the customer's path from discovering the offer to using the product. Identify the point where uncertainty or friction prevents progress.

Use a text model to organize authorized research and draft alternatives. Use analytics and spreadsheets for measured behavior, with consent and applicable privacy requirements. A vision model can critique landing-page hierarchy. See [model selection](31-models-and-workspace.md) and [data analysis](32-data-analysis-with-ai.md) before treating generated summaries as evidence.

## Build a message from evidence

Interview customers about their own words, current alternatives and objections. Write a message that connects a specific problem to a demonstrable outcome. For a learning game, “Practice health decisions in a browser, with no account required” is a checkable proposition. “Guaranteed to make every child healthier” is not.

Ask AI for three variants that preserve the same factual claims. Change one meaningful element in an experiment, such as the headline's emphasis. If you change the audience, price, design and headline together, you may not know what caused a difference. Keep a record of the version, dates, traffic source and intended outcome.

| Metric | What it can show | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | Distribution | Attention or understanding |
| Click-through rate | Response to a link or creative | Product satisfaction |
| Conversion rate | Completion under a defined denominator | Long-term customer value |
| Acquisition cost | Spend per acquired customer | Profit without retention and margin |
| Retention | Continued use | Why people stayed |

## Analyze without fooling yourself

Suppose one fictional landing page gets 20 signups from 200 visits and another gets 30 from 600. The second has more signups but a lower conversion rate. Neither result is enough to prove the design caused the difference if the traffic sources or time periods differ. Report denominators and uncertainty. Do not repeatedly peek at a small experiment and stop only when the result looks favorable.

Use AI to question your interpretation: “List alternative explanations for this conversion difference. Separate data-quality issues, audience differences and experimental-design limitations. Do not claim statistical significance without an appropriate calculation and assumptions.” Then perform the actual analysis in a reproducible tool.

## Practical project and troubleshooting

Create a campaign brief, two truthful message variants, an accessible landing page, a measurement plan and a results memo. Run a small experiment only within your authorized budget and platform rules. If you do not have real traffic, label the portfolio exercise as a simulation; never invent customer numbers.

If clicks rise but conversions fall, inspect message mismatch and page friction. If analytics disagree, reconcile event definitions, timezones, duplicate events and consent-related gaps. If AI copy sounds generic, provide actual customer language and product evidence. Avoid fake urgency, fabricated reviews, discriminatory targeting and misleading health or income claims.

Your next step is to interview one user who did not convert and one who did, with their consent. Their explanations may expose a problem no dashboard reveals. AI helps organize the evidence; strong marketing judgment connects that evidence to a better customer experience.
