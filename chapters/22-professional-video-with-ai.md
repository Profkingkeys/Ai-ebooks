# 22 · Professional Video with AI: Plan the Story Before Generating the Shot

**A cinematic opening cannot rescue a video that never explains its point.**

AI can support scripting, storyboards, captions, editing assistance and synthetic footage. A professional result still needs a clear message, coherent visual continuity, intelligible sound and accurate representation. Begin with what viewers should understand or be able to do when the video ends.

## Setup

Choose a target format and duration. Create folders for original footage, generated assets, audio, project files and exports. Keep a rights-and-consent log. Write a one-sentence purpose and a short audience description. For a product demonstration, capture the real interface when showing actual behavior; do not substitute a generated screen that implies a feature exists.

Use a text model for script alternatives, a vision model for storyboard critique and a video model for clearly appropriate synthetic scenes. Audio tools can help with noise cleanup or narration when licensing and consent permit. Model availability and output rights change, so check the provider's current terms and the [model setup guide](31-models-and-workspace.md) before production.

## Build a sixty-second demonstration

For a learning game, plan five beats: the learner's problem, entering the environment, making a choice, receiving feedback and the next useful action. Allocate time before generating assets. A storyboard should specify the shot, on-screen action, narration, sound and approximate duration.

| Beat | Visual evidence | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | A clear learner task | Establish relevance |
| Environment | Real game capture | Show what exists |
| Decision | One visible choice | Explain interaction |
| Feedback | Actual success or retry state | Demonstrate learning loop |
| Next step | Download or repository page | Help viewers try it |

Write narration that complements the image rather than describing every visible pixel. Use short sentences and leave room to absorb important actions. A prompt can ask: “Reduce this script to sixty seconds without removing the demonstrated limitation. Flag any line that the footage does not support.”

## Maintain continuity and sound quality

For generated shots, keep a reference sheet for character appearance, clothing, setting, lighting and camera direction. Generate short segments with a specific action. Inspect hands, objects, text and spatial consistency frame by frame where they matter. Avoid presenting synthetic clinical outcomes, testimonials or demonstrations as documentary evidence.

Record or generate narration only with appropriate rights and consent. Balance speech against music, avoid clipping, and listen through ordinary phone speakers as well as headphones. Create captions and correct technical terms manually. Captions are part of the product, not a final decoration.

## Challenges and solutions

A video may look sharp while teaching nothing; return to the purpose and remove unrelated shots. A generated scene may contradict the script; change the scene or wording instead of hoping viewers will miss it. Automatic captions often misread names, drug terms and code; compare them with the script and audio. Fast cuts may hide an important step; allow enough time to follow the action.

For professional delivery, verify resolution, aspect ratio, frame rate, audio, captions and safe text placement for the platform. Check the exported file from beginning to end, including the final frame. Keep a clean version without burned-in captions when the platform supports separate caption tracks.

## Practical project and outcome

Create a sixty-second product demonstration and a fifteen-second introduction using the same evidence. Deliver the editable project, final video, captions, thumbnail and asset ledger. Ask a new viewer to explain the product afterward. If they cannot, revise the story before adding more effects.

This process supports education, product onboarding, public communication and creative storytelling. AI can reduce production friction, but credibility depends on showing what is real, labeling what is synthetic and making the audience's next step clear.
