# 26 · Pitch Investors with AI: Make the Evidence Easy to Question

**An investor does not need a larger adjective. They need to understand the problem, the business and why your evidence deserves attention.**

AI can help structure a pitch, test clarity and rehearse difficult questions. It must not invent traction, partnerships, credentials, market size or financial results. A credible early-stage pitch can openly say that something is a prototype and explain the next experiment that will reduce uncertainty.

## Setup and model choice

Create an evidence folder containing the product demo, customer-discovery notes you have permission to share, actual costs, measured usage and current limitations. Separate verified facts, assumptions and goals. Use a text/reasoning model to critique the argument and a presentation tool for the final layout. Use [the model guide](31-models-and-workspace.md) to select a tool without uploading confidential investor or customer information unnecessarily.

Write the one-sentence proposition first: who has the problem, what you provide and what changes for them. Then explain the current alternative. If the audience cannot understand why the problem matters, architecture diagrams and model names will not rescue the pitch.

## Build the narrative

A concise deck can cover problem, customer, product, evidence, business model, market approach, competition, team, risks and the specific ask. The order may change with the business, but each slide should answer a real question. Show the product early enough that the audience understands what you are building.

For a simulation startup, distinguish a playable prototype from a validated curriculum product. State whether educators have reviewed it, whether learners have used it and what the evaluation measured. A software test passing is not evidence of improved educational outcomes. Present the next validation plan and the resources it requires.

| Claim | Stronger support | Weak substitute |
|---|---|---|
| The problem matters | Observed workflow and user evidence | “Everyone needs this” |
| The product works | A real demonstration | A generated concept video alone |
| Customers will pay | Actual paid pilot or clearly labeled intent | Friendly compliments |
| The market is reachable | Bottom-up customer and channel assumptions | Unsourced giant market number |
| Funding has a purpose | Milestones, budget and runway assumptions | “Scale globally” |

## Rehearse with adversarial questions

Ask AI: “Act as a skeptical but fair investor. Use only this evidence pack. Identify the weakest assumptions, ask ten specific questions and flag unsupported claims. Do not create answers from facts I have not supplied.” Answer the questions yourself and update the evidence folder.

Build a simple financial model with explicit assumptions. If a plan assumes fifty paying institutions, show how you intend to reach them, how long sales might take and what service costs accompany each customer. Use scenarios, not a single precise forecast presented as certainty. Clarify that revenue, gross margin, cash flow and valuation are different concepts.

## Practical project and outcome

Prepare a short deck, a two-minute spoken pitch, a working demo and a one-page memo. Rehearse with someone outside your specialty. Ask them to explain the business afterward. If they repeat only the technology names, revise the problem and value proposition.

Common challenges include too much technical detail, unsupported market estimates and vague funding requests. Repair them by linking every major claim to evidence, moving technical depth to an appendix and connecting the ask to measurable milestones. Do not share confidential information without an appropriate basis, and seek qualified advice before entering investment agreements.

A strong closing might explain what you have built, what you have learned and what the next funded experiment will establish. It should invite scrutiny. Your aim is a productive conversation with suitable partners, not a theatrical claim that the company is already worth a fortune.
