# Research and Evidence Method

## Claim classes

| Class | Example | Required support |
|---|---|---|
| Stable mechanism | how tokenization or authentication works | primary documentation or established textbook source |
| Empirical result | a model's measured performance | original paper, dataset, conditions, and date |
| High-stakes practice | clinical, pharmacy, legal, financial, or security guidance | authoritative source plus explicit scope and professional boundary |
| Current fact | law, policy, product capability, price, leadership | dated primary source and verification close to publication |
| Forecast | possible labor or capability change | scenario assumptions, triggers, counter-signals, and uncertainty label |

## Evidence ledger

Every nontrivial claim should eventually record:

- claim identifier
- exact chapter location
- claim class
- source and publication date
- access date
- confidence
- known disagreement or limitation
- reviewer status

## Source priority

1. standards, legislation, official documentation, and regulator guidance
2. peer-reviewed papers and original datasets
3. reproducible technical reports
4. high-quality secondary analysis
5. commentary, clearly identified as commentary

Model output is never a source.

## High-stakes review

Medicine and pharmacy chapters require clinical or pharmacy review before being labeled publication-ready. Law and finance chapters require jurisdiction and date labels. Security labs must avoid publishing operational abuse instructions that add more harm than defensive value.
