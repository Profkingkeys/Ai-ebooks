# Editorial contract

Every guide should open with a concrete tension, define its intended outcome, give setup and a practical exercise, explain likely failures and state the limits of its claims. Preserve source references and label fictional examples. Model suggestions require task-specific evaluation. Titles may be engaging but must not promise impossible or unverified outcomes.

Do not add fabricated citations, benchmark results, qualifications, income claims or clinical conclusions. Do not copy long passages from sources. Distinguish educational information from professional authority. Finish every guide with the author's three follow links.

A passing book-quality workflow establishes structural consistency and selected executable checks only. Human review remains necessary for accuracy, usefulness, accessibility and subject-specific judgment.
