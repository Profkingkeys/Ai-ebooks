# Editorial and Engineering Rules

This repository is written to feel like a serious technical book, not a generated content dump.

## Voice

- Start chapters with a problem, tension or question.
- Prefer concrete system behavior over hype.
- Explain mechanisms before conclusions.
- Use short sentences when the idea is difficult.
- Use long sentences only when they carry a precise chain of reasoning.
- Avoid empty phrases such as "revolutionary", "game changer" and "in today's world".
- Avoid heavy use of em dashes.
- Separate established facts from forecasts and proposals.
- Never turn medical, legal or financial assistance into unsupported professional advice.

## Technical discipline

Every major concept should answer four questions:

1. What is the mechanism?
2. What can fail?
3. What evidence would detect the failure?
4. What boundary prevents the failure from becoming irreversible?

## Visual discipline

A diagram should show a relationship, not decorate a page.

Preferred forms:

- workflow maps
- threat boundaries
- data lineage
- architecture graphs
- state transitions
- decision trees
- trust ladders

The reader should understand the direction of the system before reading the surrounding prose.
